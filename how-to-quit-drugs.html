A guide · drug by drug

How to quit drugs.

What stopping is actually like, in the order it usually happens, and which kinds you must never do alone. No lecture, no scare photographs, and every fact on this page has a source you can check.

Read the steps ↓ Find your drug

01 · The order it usually goes in

Stopping is six small steps, not one big one.

You do not have to be sure you want to stop to take the first one. Most people are not.

  1. 01

    Ask one question first.

    Before a hospital, before a form, before telling anybody at home: call 14446 and ask what would happen if you stopped. It is free, it answers at any hour, and it does not need your name. Asking is not agreeing to anything.

  2. 02

    Find out which kind of stopping yours is.

    Stopping ganja and stopping sleeping pills are not the same job. Some can be done at home with a bad fortnight. Some must never be done suddenly and alone. The table below says which is which.

  3. 03

    Go somewhere the Government runs or recognises.

    An Outreach and Drop-In Centre (ODIC) is the gentlest start: you walk in and leave the same day. A government or recognised centre is also the only kind Section 64A of the NDPS Act protects you at.

  4. 04

    Let them manage the withdrawal.

    Doctors call this withdrawal management. It means medicine and care for the symptoms while your body adjusts, so the worst days are shorter and safer. It is the first step, not the cure, and nobody there expects it to be.

  5. 05

    The months after are the real work.

    Cravings outlast the withdrawal. After heroin and other opioids they can last up to six months; after meth or cocaine, one to two. Counselling in those months, learning what sets you off and what to do instead, is what keeps people stopped.

  6. 06

    If you go back, you go back to step one, not to zero.

    Going back is common enough that treatment expects it. For people who keep going back to heroin or smack, there is a medicine-based treatment, opioid substitution with buprenorphine, run at government centres in India. Ask 14446 whether there is one near you.

02 · What stopping feels like

Find what you use. Read the last line twice.

From the World Health Organization’s guidance on withdrawal. It describes what usually happens, not what will happen to you, and how heavily and how long you have used changes all of it.

Hard, not dangerous

Ganja, charas, weed, hash, bhang

Cannabis

What it feels like
Usually mild, but hard to sit through: anxiety, restlessness, irritability, poor appetite, broken sleep with vivid dreams, night sweats.
How long
Lasts one to two weeks.

Safe to stop. The first fortnight of bad sleep and bad mood is the withdrawal, and it ends.

Better with help

Heroin, smack, brown sugar, chitta, afeem, doda post, codeine cough syrup, tramadol

Opioids

What it feels like
Like a very bad flu: sweating, hot and cold flushes, runny eyes and nose, cramps, diarrhoea, vomiting, no sleep. Not usually life-threatening.
How long
Starts 8 to 24 hours after the last dose and lasts 4 to 10 days. Cravings can last up to six months.

After you stop, your body can take far less than before. Going back after a gap is the most dangerous moment there is.

Never alone

Sleeping pills: alprazolam (Alprax), diazepam, nitrazepam

Benzodiazepines

What it feels like
Anxiety, sleeplessness, muscle tension and aches. In a minority of people, seizures.
How long
Starts one to two days after the last dose of a short-acting pill like alprazolam, and can last two to four weeks or longer.

Never stop suddenly on your own. A doctor brings the dose down slowly, usually a week or more between each step.

Never alone

Alcohol, every day

Alcohol

What it feels like
Shaking hands, sweating, anxiety, racing heart, no sleep, vomiting. In rare cases seizures, hallucinations and delirium.
How long
Starts 6 to 24 hours after the last drink, is worst at 36 to 72 hours, and lasts 2 to 10 days.

If you drink heavily every day, stop with a doctor, not alone. In rare cases this one can kill.

Better with help

Meth, cocaine, amphetamine

Stimulants

What it feels like
Low mood, irritability, sleeping and eating far more than usual, aches. After heavy meth use, paranoia or hallucinations, which usually fade within a week.
How long
Starts within a day and lasts 3 to 5 days. Low energy, unsteady moods and strong cravings can follow for one to two months.

The low mood is the withdrawal talking, not the truth about your life. If the thoughts turn dark, call Tele-MANAS on 14416.

Hard, not dangerous

Whitener, solution, glue, petrol

Inhalants

What it feels like
Headaches, nausea, shaking, irritability, poor concentration, sometimes hallucinations.
How long
Starts a few hours to a few days after stopping. Lasts 2 or 3 days, sometimes up to two weeks.

Thinking can stay foggy for up to a month. If school feels impossible for a few weeks, that is expected, and it passes.

Using more than one of these? Then the most careful line of the ones you use applies. Tell whoever treats you everything you take, including anything from a chemist. They need to know what is in you, not where it came from.

03 · Telling someone

The first person does not have to be your family.

It can be a counsellor on 14446 who will never see your face. It can be a doctor, who needs to know what you take, not who gave it to you. It can be one friend who knows roughly where you are on a bad night.

When you do tell someone at home, you do not need a speech. I have been using, I want to stop, and I have already found out where to go is a whole sentence, and the last part changes how the rest of it lands.

If you are under 18, the law treats you as a child who needs care, not as a criminal. What that means, with the section numbers, is on what happens if I ask.
